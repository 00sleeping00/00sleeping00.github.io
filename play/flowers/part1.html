The Wind Keeps a Garden

A garden cannot be pressed between glass. Its colour escapes, its flowers change, and the air keeps moving. This cabinet preserves a different thing: the instant in which a photograph becomes a current, and a hand persuades scattered petals to become a flower.

Victorian flower plates lend the work their patient borders and botanical names. Art Nouveau lends it the long curve of a stem, the turn of a petal, the feeling that ornament might still be growing. Each flower speaks in three colours; the wind carries the rest.

The image-flow engine follows a wind-field demo from class. Each pixel’s luminance determines the direction: luminance × 2π + wind angle. Every mark is a shaped petal, coloured exclusively with the selected flower’s three preset colours. A translucent background fades previous frames, and particles renew when their lifespan ends or they leave the image. Petals are small, with fine veins and a restrained palette. Random source sampling, the original free-flow wind field and the 0.08 trail decay are restored. No photograph or video layer is drawn beneath the particles. The source supplies luminance for movement and a slight edge emphasis; its colours never enter the palette. Closing a fist assembles the selected flower’s distinctive petal arrangement.

Load an image or enable the camera to begin. The live camera replaces the image in the main canvas, sampled in real time and mirrored to match your gestures. Stopping the camera restores the previously uploaded image, or the engraved sample if none was loaded. Loading a new photograph stops the camera. Speed, Lifespan, Wind angle and Petal count use the Demo’s ranges and defaults. Light mode uses a dark specimen field for pale petals; both themes clear the previous trails; Download PNG saves the particle canvas. Large photographs are fitted within 1280 pixels to keep the wind responsive.

Open palm: scatter. Closed fist: form the selected flower. Digits 0–9: change petal shape and colour. Mouse or touch can also guide the wind; hold the image to gather and release to scatter. “Return to image flow” restores the unmodified image vector field. Pause motion freezes the wind while preserving a visible plate. Manual controls remain selected until a different stable hand gesture takes over.

Camera recognition runs locally using the embedded MediaPipe Hand Landmarker. Use a recent Chrome or Edge browser and allow camera access. If local file camera access is restricted, open through localhost or HTTPS. No image or camera frame is uploaded. Bright, even lighting and a clearly visible palm improve recognition.
